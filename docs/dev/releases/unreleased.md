# Unreleased openPR06 changes

openPR06 has no published release yet. [v0.0.1.md](v0.0.1.md) is the draft for the first release and describes the rebase onto the openQ4 engine as of August 2026. This file collects what changed after that draft, for curation when the first release is cut. It promises no version or date. These entries are being curated as [0.15.0, the First Contact Edition](v0.15.0.md). The nightly builds on the Releases page date from April 2026, before the rebase, and contain none of this.

Engine changes merged from openQ4 are listed in openQ4's own release notes. They are inherited, not yet checked against PREY, until the [rebase status ledger](../prey-rebase/status-ledger.md) records evidence for them.

## Since 0.15.0

- **Faster startup (issue #61).** A first launch no longer brings the renderer up twice. The detected performance preset now applies as soon as the device exists, before the menus and the game load, and needs a device restart only where the window itself carries MSAA (OpenGL ES, Android, `r_glWindowMultisample 1`). That saves about 1.7 s on the reported machine. On every launch, the main-menu music's amplitude envelope is built on a worker thread rather than by decoding all of `prey_overture.ogg` during startup, about 0.35-0.6 s of `idGameLocal::Init`. The music streams and starts exactly as before. Reported, with measurements, by danperisse-hue.
- **Room reverb, spirit walk and portals sound as in retail PREY.** An audit of the sound system against retail `PREY.exe` 1.4 found these behaviours had regressed. Each room now plays the reverb its map's `efxs/<map>.efx` gives its location or area number. Before, every room played the file's `default` (for example, the Roadhouse bathrooms, and Feeding Tower B's 41 numbered areas), and loaded games now get their room reverbs back too. Spirit walk muffles the world (`s_lowPassSpirit`, -3000 mB on normal sounds) as well as lowering it by 8 dB. Sounds carry through PREY's open game portals, emerging from the far side as through a closed door. `noportalflow` keeps a portal's own hum to one side, and portals closed to sound carry none. `omniwhenclose` sounds (126 stock shaders) keep their direction outside their minimum distance. The player's landing sounds get their room reverb back: PREY's channel 10 is not Quake 4's radio channel. Voice lines sit 6 dB further back in the room. Distance falloff is quadratic again by default (`s_quadraticFalloff 1`, retail PREY's default), so distant sounds are quieter than before. `s_lowPassPortals` and `s_lowPassSpirit` are retail's cvars. See [retail-audio-reverb.md](../retail-audio-reverb.md#retail-prey-preyexe-14).
- Merged openQ4 engine work through upstream `d21bf7d4f` (27 November 2026), adapted to PREY's v7 game API. Shadow maps gain softer sunlight with distance from the caster (`r_shadowMapSunAngle`), finer near cascades and an option for cascades that keep their scale while the view turns, a cut at the light's source plane instead of collapsed cascades, skipped off-screen live casters, a larger cache for point cubes above 512, prelight caps and a far shell for distant lights (`r_shadowMapPrelightCutouts`, `r_shadowMapDistantFarShell`), and fourteen fixes from a GL and Vulkan audit. Vulkan keeps static geometry, its index lists and static stencil shadow volumes resident (`r_vkStaticGeometry` and related cvars), draws point shadow cubes as one layered target, filters each PCSS-lite tap bilinearly and keeps a stencil shadow for a point light that finds no cube. OpenGL answers its own state queries; an animated model's bounds between game tics come from its reference pose (`r_posedBoundsFromReference`); depth of field spends its samples where the blur needs them; fast-polling mice and controllers cost per frame rather than per report; Alt+Tab out of fullscreen and borderless is instant; dmap marks the areas that hold sky sides in the .proc, as Quake 4's dmap does; a light freed mid-frame stays out of the interaction table; and the engine side of openQ4's VR work arrives (headset detection with an Automatic VR Mode, fingertip presses, haptic events, the retained interface's desktop, touch and VR presentations). Several items were openPR06's own work ported to openQ4 and come back in openQ4's form: PK4 inflate through zlib-ng (now always linked statically) with a capped read-handle pool, the level-load cache writer, which now writes in place when its thread cannot start, build-0 saves, mirror clipping and the retained interface's resting pages. openPR06 keeps its own parallel modern GL shader compile (`r_rendererShaderParallelCompile`, `r_rendererShaderAllGLSLVersions`) and gains openQ4's build-time report; its first-launch preset start, which avoids openQ4's restart; and its unified `game_<arch>` module, so openQ4's CORE-07 unification and its split-module retirement do not apply. The one-time VR question at the title is not asked, because openPR06 has no VR menu page (`vr_enable` stays the switch). Not taken: openQ4's Quake 4 game code (wrist displays, hand menus, wall clearance, the unified-module game changes), its touch, tooltip and VR SETUP documents and their tests, its Steam Deck text scale, and its Quake 4 player guides. The renderer module API is 40 (openQ4's 39).
- Merged openQ4 engine work through upstream `5b74bcc5d` (24 November 2026), adapted to PREY's v7 game API. It brings video capture (`recordVideo [name]` and `stopVideo` record the game and its sound to `basepr/videos` in the save folder; `com_videoFormat`, `com_videoFPS`, `com_videoQuality`, `com_videoAudio`; MP4 uses Media Foundation on Windows, MKV, WebM, MOV and GIF need FFmpeg, and without it a recording falls back to a Motion JPEG AVI), stereoscopic 3D without a headset (`r_stereo3D`, `r_stereoSeparation`, `r_stereoConvergence`, `r_stereoSwapEyes`), a depth-of-field pass with a cheat-only camera for promotional shots (`r_dof 1` and the `r_dof*` cvars), cascaded shadow maps for large outdoor lights, Vulkan stencil shadows for a light whose shadow map does not come, BC7 normal maps that keep X in alpha, sharper texture mips while upscaling, lighter render-thread snapshots, stream refills every frame again, and an HTTPS server list read beside the master server (`net_serverListUrl`, empty by default). Performance presets now keep the native render scale: a scale an older preset lowered returns to 100% once, and the preset check reports only the frame-rate cap. The Low preset drops shadows and the light grid. Not taken: openQ4's Quake 4 game code (the weapon wheel, map-scripted depth of field, the competitive HUD, player identity, votes and the Classic ruleset), playback of retail Quake 4 render and network demos, openQ4's release qualification runner and rehearsal mode, its issue forms, and its Quake 4 player guides and tests. Multi-view demos stay gated off (`OPENPR06-GATED(D9)`), including the new client-side recording, `timeDemo` and export. The renderer module API is 39 (openQ4's 38), and the new `idNetworkSystem` methods stay outside the vtable.
- **The multiplayer screens make room for 32 players.** PREY's scoreboard listed eight players, as many as its servers took; openPR06's take 32, so everyone past the eighth (often yourself) was missing. Up to eight a list the stock board is unchanged. With more, its rows tighten into one column (up to 16 players), then the slate panel widens into two tables, with Team DM's red and blue side by side, each under its own heading strip and score. Spectators are listed, dimmed, and the footer names the game type as the menus do ("Deathmatch", not "deathmatch"). In deathmatch the HUD's top-right standings mark your own line, put you on its last line when you are outside the top four, and show your place ("12/24") beside the frag counter: Human Head's own counter, left switched off in the retail HUD. Find Servers' Players tab lists every player of the chosen server, in the stock's two boxes, which scroll past eight. See the [visual specification](../prey-ui-visual-design.md#106-multiplayer-hud-and-scoreboard). `g_forceScoreboard 1` holds the scoreboard up for scripted captures.
- **The retained menus and PREY's patched GUIs are back in the build.** The openQ4 merge of 23 November (`7ffd71925`) deleted all 153 files under `content/basepr/pak0/guis` (the retained documents, from the title to MultiPrey, and openPR06's fixes to PREY's own GUI scripts), so a fresh build presented the stock menus and the unpatched HUD. They are restored as they were before the merge.
- **openPR06 and openQ4 no longer install over each other on Windows.** The 0.15.0 installer shared openQ4's installer ID, so Windows treated the two as one program: whichever was installed second went into the other's folder and replaced its files, and both then failed to start (openQ4 reports a mismatched `pak2.pk4`). The installer now has its own ID, refuses a folder that holds openQ4, and when it updates an openPR06 0.15.0 install in place it takes over that install's Apps entry, so openQ4's installer stops choosing the folder. **If both games are broken:** uninstall them, delete the leftover folders under `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs` (saves and settings live elsewhere and are kept), then install each into its own folder with the fixed installer.
- **A fatal error on Windows now leaves a log.** Without `logFile` set, a game that stopped with an error window wrote nothing to disk, so a player had only the window's text to copy into a report. The error path now saves the message, the command line and the console window's text to `basepr\logs\fatal.log` in the save folder, and the window's last line names the file. From openQ4 `a991ad124`, merged below.
- Merged openQ4 engine work through upstream `d30280aa2` (22 November 2026). Besides the fatal error log it brings the engine side of openQ4's controller work: gyro aim (`_gyro`, `in_gyroActivation`), stick look curves, aim-assist helpers, a controller roster with first-connection notices, controller layout presets (`controllerLayout`) and an on-screen keyboard for retained text fields (`ui_onScreenKeyboard`). The layout presets and the keyboard document are Quake 4 content and are not taken, so choosing a preset other than Classic restores the Classic layout until openPR06 ships its own. Also not taken: openQ4's Quake 4 volume model (master volume to 2, ambient drones off the music slider), since openPR06 already mixes as retail PREY does; its multiplayer moderation and bot game code; and its Quake 4 tests and documents. `idNetworkSystem::GetClientAddress` stays outside the vtable, which Prey's v7 game API fixes.
- **Multiplayer deaths show as a graphical feed.** Kills, suicides and telefrags draw at the HUD's top left as "killer [icon] victim" lines, built like the HUD's top-right standings box, instead of chat lines: up to five at once, three seconds each, lines naming you lit as your scoreboard row is. Each weapon has its own compact silhouette (wrench, spirit bow, Hunter rifle, crawler, auto cannon, acid sprayer, rocket launcher, leech gun), with a corner badge for an alternate fire (the rifle's scope, a crawler trap, grenades, the leech gun's plasma, bolt and sun) and a skull for a suicide; drawn for openPR06 by `tools/ui/obituary_icons.py`. The console and the in-game menu's chat history keep the text. `hud_graphicalObits 0` restores the chat lines. A mod's projectile can name its own icon with `mtr_obituary` and `mtr_obituaryBadge`. See the [visual specification](../prey-ui-visual-design.md#121-current-additions).
- **A weapon wheel.** Hold Q (or a controller's LB) to see Tommy's seven weapons on a ring, point at one and let go to equip it; a quick tap swaps back to the previous weapon. It is PREY's weapon bar bent round a ring, with the bar's own icons, ammo lines and selector, and the world goes out of focus behind your raised weapon while it shows. Single player slows while it is open; multiplayer keeps its speed. Escape, Back or a second press cancels; a weapon key, next and previous choose on it. It follows openQ4's wheel, written for PREY's player. Options gain a Weapon Wheel binding and Wheel Offset and Wheel Sensitivity rows (Game Options -> HUD); the default controller bindings move Previous Weapon from LB to the D-pad alone. See the [input guide](../../user/input-settings.md#weapon-wheel) and the [visual specification](../prey-ui-visual-design.md#121-current-additions).
- **Examining a new weapon pulls the focus onto it.** While Tommy turns a newly acquired weapon over in his hands (its `initialPickup` animation), the world behind it blurs and the weapon and his hands stay sharp; the focus lets go as the weapon comes up. The game asks openQ4's depth of field (OpenGL and Vulkan) for a thin lens focused on the weapon with `VIEW_DOF_SHARP_VIEW_WEAPON`, through `idRenderSystem::SetViewDepthOfField` (render API 39). `r_depthOfField 0` turns it off and `r_depthOfFieldRadius` sets the widest blur; both are rows under Options > Video > Advanced FX > Depth of Field. See [display settings](../../user/display-settings.md#depth-of-field).

## Highlights

- **The project is now openPR06.** It was openPREY, which carried the full PREY trademark in its name. Executables, packages, the save folder, the settings file, environment variables (`OPENPR06_*`, formerly `OPENPREY_*`), the repository URL (`https://github.com/themuffinator/openPR06`) and the shipped fonts' names all use the new name. The first run moves an existing `openPREY` save folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\openPREY`, `~/.local/share/openprey`, `~/Library/Application Support/openPREY`) to the new name, or keeps using it if the move fails. `openPREYConfig.cfg`, `openPREY RDEMO` render demos and the `requiredopenPREYVersion` mod.json key are still read; `OPENPREY_*` environment variables are not. A new logo, after openQ4's and openD3's, shows "PR" with a small superscript "06" on a teal plate crossed by the hawk Talon's insignia; the README banner is that logo on a transparent ground, and a new social preview (`assets/docs/img/social-preview.jpg`) sets it over a rendered view of the Sphere, an out-of-focus Earth built from NASA's public-domain maps, and space. `tools/assets/build_banner.py` renders both.
- **The game library is in this repository.** The PREY SDK-derived trees, `src/game/`, `src/Prey/` and `src/preyengine/`, were imported with their history from the former `OpenPrey-game` companion repository. Meson builds the unified `game_<arch>` module in place, so no companion checkout, configure-time staging or `OPENPR06_GAMELIBS_REPO` is needed. This supersedes the developer upgrade note in the 0.0.1 draft. The game trees stay under the PREY SDK EULA and the engine stays GPLv3; see [LICENSING.md](../../../LICENSING.md) and the [game source provenance record](../game-source-provenance.md).
- **Multiplayer bot characters (experimental).** Nineteen named bots built on PREY's own artificial player (`hhArtificialPlayer`), each with one of six fighting styles, a retail player model and its own chat lines, added with `addbot [character] [skill]`. Retail maps ship no bot navigation, so bots fight what they can see rather than planning routes. See the [bot guide](../mp-bots.md).
- **A new body text face, Talon UI Condensed.** Setting rows, buttons, lists, dialogs and HUD text are drawn in Talon UI Condensed (`fonts/talon-ui-condensed.ttf`), a face drawn for openPR06 in place of the tracing of PREY's retail body font: Impact's condensed frame with the squared bowls of the PREY wordmark, a step lighter (stems 0.19 of the capital height against 0.215) and with counters about twice as open, so small text stays legible. It sets within 1.2% of the old face's width, so the stock and retained screens keep their layout. It covers the same characters as before: Talon's own drawings for Latin, Greek and Russian and Ukrainian Cyrillic, with weight-matched Noto for the rest. It replaces only the narrow body face; the display, alien and console faces are unchanged. Languages other than the Western ones folded onto English use it for body text too; a localised install that ships its own body-font atlases had kept the retail face. It is licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, which ships in `licenses/`. `r_useTrueTypeFonts 0` still selects the retail bitmaps. See [ttf-font-system.md](../ttf-font-system.md#talon-ui-condensed-the-body-face).
- **A reorganised Options page in the retained interface (`ui_retained 1`).** Each page now holds two columns of compact rows under section headings, so the page reaches the settings openPR06 had added without a row: new Mouse and Controller tabs (acceleration, always run, toggle crouch, stick dead zone, look and move curves, invert look, trigger press point, rumble, gyro, touchpad, button prompt style, swap confirm and back), Game Options' HUD, Accessibility and System tabs (field of view, weapon field of view, player shadow, blood and effects detail, gameplay tips, FPS counter, chat layout, missing-caption marks, double vision, underwater warp, reduced motion, opaque dialogs, text backing, 3D menu backdrop, console key, screenshot format, logo videos, update check), and new Video and Audio rows (upscaling, shadow map size and filtering, soft particles, temporal AA, bloom threshold, motion blur strength, glow strength, dialogue volume, occlusion, air absorption, output limiter, mute when unfocused). Bound keys are drawn as keycap, mouse and controller glyphs; the bottom bar shows Back, Accept, Clear and Reset prompts for the device in use; the bumpers and triggers page the tabs. Controllers have default bindings (`basepr/openpr06_bindings.cfg`), which Reset Defaults restores. Adaptive Difficulty and Field of View are now saved, and Chromatic Aberration's slider covers the setting's real range. See the [visual specification](../prey-ui-visual-design.md#options).
- **Controller buttons in the game's tips.** The big key plates of the campaign's tutorial tips ("C to crouch"), the shuttle and rifle-scope tips and the multiplayer ready and vote tips show the button of the controller in use, drawn in the style of PREY's keycap and mouse plates, with each family's labels (Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo, Steam Deck or a generic layout). A tip on screen changes when the player switches device. The Controls page now shows and keeps up to three keys and three controller buttons per action, and the defaults bind the share button to Screenshot and give Walk/Run (Caps Lock) and Zoom (Z) keyboard keys. See the [visual specification](../prey-ui-visual-design.md#121-current-additions).
- **The retained interface is the default (`ui_retained 1`).** The title, its pages, the pause and death screens, Options, the system check, the loading screen, MultiPrey, the connecting screen, the message boxes and the multiplayer chat line now present as retained documents unless `ui_retained 0` selects PREY's stock menus; the HUD stays PREY's own. Configurations saved before this stored `ui_retained 0` and are moved to 1 once (`ui_retainedDefaultMigrated`); a startup `+set ui_retained` is left alone, and a later `ui_retained 0` sticks. openQ4 already defaults it to 1, so the engine's declaration matches upstream again.
- **Damaged retail Ogg Vorbis files play through.** Sounds that stop early under stb_vorbis are decoded again with libvorbisfile (libogg 1.3.6 and libvorbis 1.3.7 Meson wraps), which resumes after a bad page instead of truncating the sound.

## Engine

- Merged openQ4 engine work through upstream `d3056567a` (21 November 2026), adapted to PREY's v7 game API. Earlier syncs brought the extended graphics presets with Auto-Detect, the classic menus laid out like the new interface, cached shadow casters and projected shadow maps on Vulkan, and display settings that apply without a device restart.
- The November 17 sync adds, from openQ4's engine: PNG textures and PNG screenshots (`r_screenshotFormat 1`); texture packs that replace `downsize()` inputs, decode BC7, and rebuild bump maps from replaced inputs, compressed; Vulkan textures reduced to fit video memory, and loading that stops on a lost device; OpenGL MSAA changes without a new window, OpenGL's post-processing chain cut to Vulkan's (half-resolution SSAO, no scene copies) and its scene target cleared each frame; optional character self-shadows under shadow maps (`r_shadowMapCharacterSelfShadow 1`, off by default), softer modern-OpenGL shadows (PCSS-lite), steadier still-light shadow caches and light-interaction cull bits computed in one pass; render demos recorded in checked blocks with keyframes (about 40% of the size, one-second seeks); network protocol 2.42 (keyed channel packets, server info and connect replies that fit one 1280-byte packet, snapshots replayed between tics, commands kept on time through long client frames, bounded client floods); a log file that stops logging instead of crashing when it cannot be written; and a mod's string tables read on top of openPR06's own.
- The November 20 sync adds: BC4, BC5 and BC6H (HDR) DDS textures, BC6H decoded to 16-bit floating point where the renderer lacks BPTC, and big texture packs that stay compressed; cascaded sunlight on the Quality and Ultra presets as well as Maximum, with the light's whole map kept past the cascades' reach and the normal offset of distant lights sized at each receiver's depth; OpenGL point-light shadow cubes drawn into one layered target; cheaper faded decals, particle expiry and idle reverb slots; a pack found under two search roots mounted only once; and the name of the loose file that makes a pack's DDS look stale.
- The November 21 sync adds: startup and loading screens that animate at the display's refresh rate in even steps, the startup screen held until the first loading screen takes over and the level's first view drawn unseen before the loading screen hands over; reloaded textures decoded on worker threads and a texture setting's reload spread across frames, touching only the textures it reaches; the froxel volumetric haze (`r_rendererFroxelVolumetrics`) lit by shadow-mapped lights; HBAO (`r_ssaoMethod 1`) and ground-truth ambient occlusion (`r_ssaoMethod 2`) beside SSAO, whose filter no longer dithers; parallax occlusion mapping for PBR height maps; volume and texture array images and sixteen `shaderTexture` slots per GLSL material stage, with stages sharing one program; and retained interface pages that no longer lay out or recompile every frame at rest. Not taken: its Quake 4 game code (the medic and tech combat-stance fix, the `si_name` default), its Quake 4 menus and their Ambient Occlusion and multiplayer rows, its multiplayer player guide, and the capture harnesses that need Quake 4 maps.
- The menu's Updates check now asks openPR06's GitHub releases (openQ4's in-game updater) instead of the retail master server, which never knew openPR06 builds. openPR06 publishes no update manifest or signing key yet, so the check reports that it could not reach an update and offers the releases page; it never installs anything. `openPR06-updater_<arch>` is staged beside the client for when releases carry signed manifests.
- Not taken from that sync: its Quake 4 game code (Instagib, the Classic ruleset and grappling hook, buying, the map database and multiplayer modifiers, enhanced physics, weapon-fire shadows, the camera-kick option, the marines' flashlights) and its Quake 4 and Awakening content. The retained interface's new menu sound cue stream does not play openQ4's stock cue sounds, which PREY does not declare; openPR06's retained documents name their own PREY sounds, and the stream drives openPR06's quiet hover and step cues instead (below).
- Rebase hardening: guards for the render-demo header, the network rate defaults (32000), the GUI stream marker and window-flag layout, and the loading-music declaration scope; the missing autosave string `#str_107240`; lazy loading of dormant ARB programs; the particle deform implementation; and image alias handling.
- Shadow maps are the default shadow technique (`r_useShadowMap 1`) on every preset that draws shadows, Low, Performance and Balanced included; stencil volumes remain the per-light fallback. TrueType text is the default (`r_useTrueTypeFonts 1`). Configurations saved with the old values are moved to the new defaults once (`r_preyDefaultsMigrated`).
- Shadow maps of parallel lights, distant point lights and projectors store the triangles a stencil shadow is built from. They stored the opposite side, so in the Land of the Ancients maps the sun shone through canyon walls that stencil shadows: 5.7% of lotaa's start view and 12.4% of lotab's differed from the stencil picture, now 1.5% and 1.6% (shadow edges), on OpenGL and Vulkan. See the [shadow-mapping audit](../shadowmapping-audit-2026-11-09.md#correction-14-november-the-handedness-was-the-wrong-way-round-openpr06).
- Levels load over their own loading art again, as in retail PREY: single-player levels showed the generic painting and multiplayer levels their 512 menu thumbnail. A new client-only content pack, `pak2.pk4` (275 MB), replaces all 33 in-game loading screens with recaptures taken at the retail cameras, recovered from the retail art, rendered with shadow maps for every light and extended with the tiles that fill wide and tall windows. `feedingtowera`'s, the ship over roadhouse's torn-off roof, is captured in a staged copy of roadhouse's ending ([levelshot capture notes](../levelshot-capture.md)). `levelshot` writes PREY's `guis/assets/loading/<map>` by default.
- Surfaces drawn by PREY's own per-light shader programs (skin, cloth, hair, parallax, masked and the organic walls) take their shadows from a shadow-mapped light's map on OpenGL, with the same soft edges as everything else; they used the light's stencil volumes (`r_shadowMapProgramReceivers`, default 1).
- Beam effects are visible again: forcefield, laser and energy beams whose material turns them to the viewer (`deform beam`) were drawn edge-on and vanished. The beam model now builds retail PREY's ribbon, with the texture running once along the whole beam rather than once per segment.
- PREY's heat-haze, refraction and membrane programs keep their distortion under an off-axis projection (a levelshot's side tiles, a VR eye); it swelled into a smear there.
- A `levelshot` source larger than the window, rendered in regions, now matches a one-pass render, so supersampling is no longer capped at the window size. Each region fitted cascaded shadow maps to its own narrow frustum, and a shadowed surface could come out lit (in `keeperfortress`, a wall panel behind the glass and a wedge of the window frame); regions now use their whole tile's cascades, and the distortion programs' deform keeps the whole view's clamp and per-axis scale.
- Vulkan draws PREY's glow overlay: every `glowStage` material stage is redrawn into a black copy of the view, blurred per axis with `r_glowSteps`, `r_glowAlpha` and `r_glowAlphaChange`, and added over the scene at `r_glowStrength`, as the OpenGL renderer does.
- OpenGL's glow overlay follows retail PREY: the glow stages are reduced to a 256x256 image, blurred there with taps weighted by the square of the blur alpha, and stretched over the view. It was blurred at full size with taps weighted by the alpha itself, which drew glow about four times too strong and a seventh as wide, so the multiplayer maps' hexagonal wall-walk panels turned into thick white outlines and energy effects into opaque bursts. The glow also includes the stages of a view's portal sky, as retail's re-rendered glow view did; the overlay is skipped in views with no glow stage. Vulkan's overlay is unchanged.
- Fog and blend lights affect only surfaces with lighting stages, as in retail PREY (Doom 3's rule); they took ambient-only surfaces too (Quake 4's `noFog` rule), so a black fog in `dmshuttle1` painted the sky portal over its Earth window black.
- Classic GUI tabs now send the commands their `onTabActivate` scripts raise when the tab is switched to. Prey's menus get back the tab-flip sound and the scans each tab starts: player models on Player Settings, maps on Create Server, servers on Find Servers and control profiles on Profiles.
- A classic GUI script's `namedEvent` now sends the commands that the windows answering it raise, while the GUI is active. Opening MultiPrey refreshes the server list and checks for updates, and Options' Apply Changes (or leaving Options with a pending change) restarts the video mode.
- World GUIs now send the commands they raise when the game gives them a named event, as retail PREY did. A console that an AI uses triggers its targets and plays its sound, scripted consoles play their sounds, the roadhouse games reset, and the jukebox turns on and off.
- The mix follows retail PREY's loudness. The score (`SC_MUSIC`) takes the music volume in place of the master volume, and every other sound takes the master volume alone. Both default to retail's 0 dB. Quake 4's speaker attenuation (`s_speakerFraction`), which put every positioned sound 3.7 dB (6 dB in older configs) under the score, is gone. Dialogue that asks for it ducks the rest of the mix by 4.5 dB again (`s_voiceDucking`), so the Roadhouse jukebox dips under Tommy's lines. The jukebox and the level-load music now follow the master volume, as in retail, and `s_volume 0` no longer silences the score.
- The server browser's status line and scan progress have openPR06 strings in English, French, Italian and Spanish. Retail Prey's language files have none, so a scan logged an "Unknown string id" warning every frame, and the server count and mod filter showed bare string IDs.
- A server's details in the browser are labelled in English, French, Italian and Spanish: map, time and frag limits, team damage, team balance, pure and spectators. A passworded server reads "PASSWORD REQUIRED", as in retail PREY. The Quake 4 game types' labels no longer show lines of Tommy's dialogue.
- The classic GUI pointer is retail PREY's size: 16 canvas units on the menus (it was 15) and 32 on world GUIs (it was Doom 3's 64, twice retail's). `openq4_guiCursor <x> <y>` places a classic GUI's pointer for scripted captures.
- The in-game and system consoles use the PREY palette instead of Quake 4 amber and olive: the in-game console keeps a black field with portal-cyan text and edge, and the system console is dark Sphere-shell slate.
- Windows builds show the startup splash, as openQ4 does: a 512x384 borderless window, centred while the engine loads and closed when the game window opens. The Windows resource script never embedded `assets/splash/prey_rt_bitmap_4001.bmp`, so the splash had nothing to show.
- A first retained (vector) replacement of the main menu's navigation, off by default: `ui_retained 1` draws the home screen's arc of hooks, bottom bar and the New Game, Exit and Quit Game frames as resolution-independent vectors over the stock portal backdrop, out of game and in a single-player game. Load Game and Save Game are rebuilt the same way at the stock layout, with the saves table, the save's picture, the name field and the stock confirmations before deleting, overwriting or, in a game, loading. They add what the stock menu lacks: keyboard and controller navigation with a visible focus, and the whole row as the pointer's target. The death screen's frame and its Load, Restart and Main Menu buttons are rebuilt too, over the stock backdrop and fade. Mods is a frame of the title with the installed mods and Load MOD, and Credits is the stock roll, line for line, in a retained page. ## Build and CI

- `openpr06-validation.yml` builds the engine, dedicated server, OpenGL and Vulkan renderers and the unified game module as debug builds on Windows x64 and ARM64, Linux x64 and ARM64, macOS ARM64 (OpenGL and Metal bridge) and macOS Intel x64 (OpenGL), then checks the staged payload. The ARM64 lanes build natively on the same `windows-11-arm` and `ubuntu-24.04-arm` runners as the release, so ARM64 breakage shows up on the push rather than at release time. The Linux lanes install their native development packages, the Windows and macOS lanes prepare an OpenAL Soft runtime before staging (cached between pushes on Windows), and the macOS lanes validate the staged binaries as the release lanes do.
- Fixed debug-only build failures in the game library on MSVC x64 and GCC, and header spellings that resolved only on case-insensitive filesystems.
- VS Code launch configurations come in OpenGL and Vulkan variants and cover the deathwalk maps.

## Documentation

- The player guides now describe openPR06 only. Guides for Quake 4-only features carried in by engine merges were removed: The Awakening, campaigns, the Arena Campaign, competitive matches, liquids, classic dynamic lights, Android, the level editor and languages. The remaining inherited guides say where PREY testing is still pending.

## Known Limitations

- Full campaign completion and multiplayer parity aren't claimed yet.
- Multi-view demos and the demo repeater are compiled out (`OPENPR06_ENABLE_MVD=0`, `OPENPR06_ENABLE_REPEATER=0`), along with openQ4's engine-side bot integration.
- ModernGL and Vulkan feature parity with PREY's original interaction stages is deferred; OpenGL stays the default.
- Checksums for the CD-era numbered `pak000.pk4`–`pak004.pk4` layout aren't recorded yet; those packs are checked for presence only.
- The inherited openQ4 macOS Debug, macOS Sanitizer, Universal2 and Linux ARM64 cross workflows stay switched off until they're rebuilt for openPR06; macOS builds come from active CI and the release lanes instead.
